= SaaS Foundations 01

== The SaaS Business Model

=== What is SaaS?

SaaS(Software as a Service) is a delivery and billing model for software

Instead of selling a software license as a one-time product, the provider generally:

. continously develops the software

. runs it on infastructure they control

. delivers it over the internet

. and receives recurring revenue while customers continue using it

SaaS therefore creates an ongoing relationship between the software company and the customer.

=== Why SaaS works

*For customers*

SaaS generally:

. requires little/no local installation

. shifts mantenance and infrastructer responsibility to the provider

. reduces problems caused by customers' different hardware/configurations

. can appear cheaper initially than purchasing a traditional software license

*For Developers*

The major advantage is actually the delivery model, not just subscriptions.

Because the company usually controls the environment where the software runs, deployment is more consistent, updates can be continous, fewer customer-specific environments need support.

*For the business*

SaaS revenue is generally recurring and more predictable than repeatedly selling individual licenses.

Predictable future revenue also makes it possible for businesses to invest money today based on expected future cash flow.

=== SaaS Sales Models

Stripe divides SaaS primarily into low-touch and high-touch sales models

This distinction can affect the company more fundamentally than B2B vs. B2C or even the technology used.

==== Low-touch SaaS

Low-touch SaaS is desgined so most customers can discover, evaluate, purchase, and use the software without sustained one-on-one human interaction.

Typical system:

Website / marketing
        ↓
Free trial / signup
        ↓
Self-service onboarding
        ↓
Product usage
        ↓
Paid subscription

This requires scalabale systems such as low friction signup/onboarding, automated marketing, documentation, product design that prevents support problems, and scalabale customer support.

A Customer success team may help users successfully adopt the product rather than primarily convincing them to buy.

Low-touch companies primarily focus on:

*MRR* - Monthly Recurring Revenue

because customers commonly pay monthly.

==== High-touch SaaS

High-touch SaaS uses substantial human involvement to convince customers to adopt the product, implement it successfully, and continue using it.

Typical system:

Find prospect
     ↓
Sales process
     ↓
Evaluation / meetings
     ↓
Agreement
     ↓
Implementation
     ↓
Ongoing account management

Some common roles include:

. SDR - Sales Development Representative: finds prospects

. AE - Account executive: manages/closes the sale

. AM - Account Manager: manages the existing customer relationships

High-touch SaaS is overwhelmingly B2B and often involves more expensive contracts

These buisnesses commonly focus on:

ARR - Annua recurring revenue

Because contracts and revenue are often considered annually.

=== Low-touch vs. high-touch is not the same as B2C vs B2B

B2B/B2C: who are you selling to?

Low-touch/high-touch: How do you sell them?

For example, B2B software can still be low-touch if businesses purchase it through a self-service process.

Important: The sales model affects product design, pricing, marketing, support, and company strucutre. A mismatch between the product/market and its sales model can be extremely damaging.

=== Hybrid sales

Some companies combine elements of both models. For example, a low touch-company might add customer-success staff that resemble salespeople

But successfully operating both complete models simultaneously for the same product is uncommon becasue each requires very different company operations

=== The fundamental SaaS Model

This is the central idea:

Long-term Saas revenue depends on:

Acquisiton * Converion * Revenue per customer * How long customers remain.

=== APRU - Average Revenue Per User

APRU = Average revenue generated by an account during a given period

Example:

Average customer pays 30/month, so your APRU is 30/month

=== Churn

Churn = precentage of exisitng customers who stop paying during a given period

So if in one month you lose 10 of your 200 customers, you have a 5% monthly churn.

Churn is especially important in SaaS because the business depends on customers continuing to pay.

High churn means customers disappear before producing much recurring revenue.

=== LTV - lifetime Value

LTV = expected revenue produced by a customer over their entire relationship with the company.

A 30/month customer isnt economically worth only their first 30 dollars, but rather the stream of future payments they may produce while remaining a customer

=== CAC - Customer Acquisition Cost

CAC = marketing/sales spending required to acquire a new customer

The important relationship is:

Spend money acquiring customer now
                ↓
Customer produces revenue over time
                ↓
Lifetime revenue must justify acquisition cost

Because acquisition expenses happen early while subscription revenue arrives later, rapidly growing SaaS businesses can require substantial upfront capital.

=== SaaS improvements compound

The major SaaS variables multiply, so improving multiple variables compounts their effects

For example acquisiton improvement * conversion improvment creates the product of combined improvement.

=== Why SaaS growth eventually plateaus

If acquisition, conversion, and churn remain fixed, eventually:

new customers gained = existing customers lost

and growth plateaus

Therefore, continued growth eventually requires improving some part of the system.

A company that reaches this plateu before earning enough revenue to cover its costs can fail even if the product itself functions well.

=== SaaS economics

Software has unusual economics because once software exists, serving another customer can often be relatively inexpensive.

The company may spend far more on sales, customer acquisition, engineering/product development, and general business operations, rather than on creating another copy of the software

This makes customer acquisiton economics especially important.

=== Bootstrapped vs. funded SaaS

These businesses can haev very different definitions of healthy growth

==== Bootrsatpped

Bootstrapped primarily grows using founder resources and/or revenue generated by the business

Sustainable profitiability may therefore matter relatively early.

==== Funded

Funded uses outside investment to finance growth. So a funded SaaS company may intentionally spend heavily early to acquire plentiful customers to then profit from later.

=== Benchmarks need context

Stripe provides benchmark conversion rates, churn rates, sales productivity figures, etc, but the bigger lesson is:

*There is no universally "good" SaaS number.*

Benchmarks vary within the industry, business model, company stage, customer type, and general goals.

Even a metric becoming worse dosent automatically mean the business worsened


=== Product-Market Fit

Product-market fit (PMF) is the state where a product strongly satisfied the need of a particular market/customer group.

before strong PMF, companies commonly experience:

. Weak conversion
. High churn
. Difficult sales
. Uncertain positioning/product

Finding PMF generally requires iteration rather than simply launching once and having it immediately

Customer conversations ar eparticularly important because founders need to understand why customers do or do not adopt, use, and continue paying for the product.
